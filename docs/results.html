Every message carries the capture time of its buffer's first sample, propagated unchanged. ContactPrediction also carries start_sample_index, so a prediction can be traced to the exact window that produced it across independently timed runs.

Spectrogram contract

The filterbank is published unpadded and unnormalised. AST pads to 1024 rows and then normalises, so its padded rows arrive as a uniform 0.4670 — 90.4% of every tensor it sees. Publishing that padded form would put 524 KB on the wire per message to carry the same 98 rows.

Cost of running it

The DAQ node pins torch to a single thread. Left at torch's default it spent 18.19 ms of CPU per 2.99 ms of work — six cores to do one core's job — because a 98 × 128 filterbank is far too small to parallelise and the worker threads spin-wait between calls. Single-threaded is both faster in wall time (2.18 ms) and about five times cheaper. Measured in the running node: 341% → 40% CPU. This matters on the robot, where the DAQ must not compete with the control stack.

Environment, not the pipeline

On this laptop the node reports a ~1.1 s late publish tick every 33.32 s, exactly periodic and phase-locked across process restarts, with or without image publishing. Something outside ROS stalls the WSL VM on a fixed cycle; the node's late-tick instrumentation is correctly reporting someone else's stall rather than its own. Re-measure on the lab machine before drawing conclusions from it.

What these numbers do and do not show

  • Pretraining contributes. Pretrained AST beats the random control by 9.3 percentage points per window at identical compute cost — evidence, for this dataset and one fixed seed, that AudioSet pretraining is doing real work rather than the architecture alone.
  • The CNN wins on both axes here — best per-window result while sustaining 15 Hz — but it is trained on ~81 windows from 9 clips per fold and will be overfitting.
  • Mel statistics beat AST per window (101 vs 99) at roughly 1900× lower isolated latency, with zero parameters.
  • Per-clip accuracy is saturated at 12/12 for everything except the control, so it cannot distinguish AST, CNN and mel statistics. Per-window is the only discriminating figure.
  • This is 12 clips with 50%-overlapping windows. The 108 windows are not 108 independent samples. These results demonstrate performance on a small, well-separated dataset — not general superiority over AST.

Fold isolation

Fixed extractors (ast, ast_random, melstats) cannot learn from this dataset, so each window is encoded once and split per fold. The CNN is retrained inside every fold on that fold's training clips only — training it once over all windows and then cross-validating the centroids would leak the held-out trial into the encoder. Centroids always come from the fold's training clips. Fold artifacts are written to eval_runs/; three independent mechanisms stop one reaching the installed package.

Timing: classification yes, synchronisation no

Open acceptance item

WSLg supports live demonstration and 15 Hz inference.
WSLg microphone timestamps are unsuitable for robot-state synchronisation.
Native lab hardware must pass the timing gate before recording synchronised bags.

The DAQ maps PortAudio's inputBufferAdcTime onto the ROS clock per callback, so a window stamp means when the sound reached the ADC. That fixed a real defect: window age went from −613 ms (stamps in the future) to a median of 1047 ms against a 1000 ms buffer.

What remains is the backend, not the derivation. Across 70-second microphone runs the WSLg PulseAudio bridge disagreed with its own sample counter — and did so differently each run:

CriterionWAV controlWSLg microphoneVerdict
no future window stamps00pass
stamps strictly monotonic0 bad0 badpass
window age near 1 s989 ms1047 mspass
stamp Δ matches sample-index Δ0.000 ms5.8 – 2275 msfail
sample-clock driftn/a−25 to −354 ppmfail
capture discontinuities > 5 msn/a2 – 683fail
PortAudio status eventsn/a0pass

In the worst run the median callback interval was 25.70 ms where 1024 frames at 44 100 Hz implies 23.22 ms — an 11% disagreement, with 683 jumps up to 2473 ms. PortAudio reported zero overflows throughout: the drops are invisible to the backend, which is why the gate measures them independently.

The identical gate against the WAV source passes every applicable criterion with a worst stamp-versus-index delta of 0.000 ms. The derivation and the measurement harness are therefore sound; the failure is specific to the WSLg microphone path.

Consequence

Bags recorded through WSLg cannot be aligned to /joint_states, which is the whole point of the timestamp discipline. Run verify_sprint.sh --mic on the lab machine and require it to pass before any synchronised data collection. Do not relax --max-drift-ppm or --max-discontinuities to make a known-bad backend pass; set them from the synchronisation requirement.
